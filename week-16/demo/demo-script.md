# Week 16 Demo — Presentations 🎤

**The last night · one project at a time · then home**

Tonight there is nothing to type. Each student presents the project they started in week 4, from their own laptop, on the projector: it runs, they show what it does, they explain one decision and why, and they say what's next. You call the order, keep the time, ask one question, and fill in the grading sheet. When the last one finishes, you close, and the class ends.

> **The shape of the night:** opening → presentations → close. **No lab, no starter, no checks.**

**Total: about five minutes of you talking, plus ten minutes for each presentation.** The lesson plan's timing table has the rest.

---

## 0 · Before class

- [ ] **Check the published sheet is current.** Open [the hosted cue sheet](https://jgrissom.github.io/dotnet-db-dev/week-16/demo/script.html) and confirm the top line says *the last night*. If it doesn't, the Pages deploy is behind — **the markdown is the truth**
- [ ] ⚠️ **The grading sheet, printed** — the lesson plan has it, one row per student
- [ ] ⚠️ **A laptop connects to the projector**, with the cable or adapter the room has. Try one before anyone arrives
- [ ] ⚠️ **The `Final Presentation` rubric is set up in Canvas**: four criteria of 10
- [ ] **The order of presenters, written down**
- [ ] **A timer** where you can see it and the presenter can't

---

## 1 · Opening

- [ ] **No slides tonight.** The projector is for the students' programs
- [ ] 📖 *"Tonight you present your project. It runs from start to finish, you show what it does, you explain one decision you made and why, and you say what you'd do next. That's the whole night."*
- [ ] 📖 *"About ten minutes each, from your own laptop. At nine minutes I'll say one minute. At the end I'll ask you one question."*
- [ ] 📖 **Read out the order.** *"Here's the order. When it's your turn, bring your laptop up and plug it in."*

---

## 2 · Presentations

- [ ] **For each student, in order:**
  1. **Call their name.** They plug in. Start the timer when their program is on the screen
  2. **At nine minutes**, say *"One minute."*
  3. 🎯 **When they finish, ask one question** about the decision they explained: *"Why that, and not something else?"*
  4. **Fill in their row of the grading sheet** while the next person plugs in: 10 per part, and a note for any part that lost points
- [ ] ⚠️ **A program that won't start:** give them a minute to fix it. Then they do the other three parts from the code and the README

---

## 3 · Close

- [ ] 📖 *"That's everyone. Your project and its repo are yours to keep. Grades go into Canvas. Thank you."*
- [ ] **Then the class ends.** After everyone leaves, put the grading sheet's totals into Canvas's `Final Presentation` rubric
