# Week 16 — Lesson Plan

**Topic:** Final project presentations, one student at a time, each about ten minutes. Then the class ends.
**Session length:** 3h 45m, of which only the presentations and the close are used

> **Nothing new tonight.** Each student presents the project they started in week 4, live, from their own laptop: it runs, they show what it does, they explain one decision and why, and they say what's next. **40 points, four parts of 10**, the same table students have seen in the syllabus and in week 14. **There is no lab, no starter, and no checks.** In a 15-week term this is the last class instead of week 16, and nothing in it depends on week 15.

## 🎯 The payoff moment — the demo's

**Each project, running live.** A registry a student named in week 4 now saves to a database, refuses bad input, and keeps a second table, and its owner explains why one piece of it is the way it is. Nothing is said about it from the front: the student runs it.

## 🎯 The payoff moment — the lab's

**There is no lab tonight.** The presentations are the students' keyboard time, on their own projects.

## Instructor notes

- **Ask one question at the end of each presentation**, about the decision they explained: *"Why that, and not something else?"* It's the part of the grade nobody else could have written for them.
- **Keep to about ten minutes.** Give a one-minute warning at nine.
- **Grading happens on paper, during the night**, and goes into Canvas's `Final Presentation` rubric afterwards. Nothing is machine-checked.
- **Students may watch each other.** Nobody has seen anyone else's presentation before tonight: week 15's practice was done alone.

## What could go wrong

| If | Then |
|---|---|
| A laptop won't connect to the projector | Let them present from their own screen, with you beside them, or swap to a lab PC (they need their connection string set again: week 10's *A lab PC that rebooted*) |
| The program won't start: no connection, or a crash | Give them a minute to fix it, then let them do the other three parts from the code and the README. The first part is the one at stake |
| The college's server or the network is down | Every program fails at the same step. Decide that night whether to reschedule *it runs*; the other three parts still happen |
| A student is absent | The syllabus says what it's worth and nothing more. Your call |
