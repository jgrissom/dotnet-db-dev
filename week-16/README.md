# Week 16 — Presentations

The last night of the course. **Each student presents their semester project, live, for 40 points**, and then the class ends. There is no lab, no new material, and nothing to copy in. In a 15-week term, this is the last class instead.

## Use in this order

| When | Document | What it is |
|------|----------|------------|
| Prep | 🗓️&nbsp;[lesson-⁠plan.md](lesson-plan.md) | The night's running order, and a grading sheet to print |
| In&nbsp;class | 🎨&nbsp;[demo/⁠](demo/) | *Presentations* — the cue sheet for the night; [clickable cue sheet](https://jgrissom.github.io/dotnet-db-dev/week-16/demo/script.html) |

## What to bring

- **Your laptop, charged, with your project on it.** You present from your own machine, on the projector, for about ten minutes.
- **Your project's `README.md`, with its three notes** — *what it does*, *one decision, and why*, *what's next*. You wrote them in week 14.
- **Run your program once before class, on that laptop.** Your connection string is in your user secrets, on that machine only. Presenting from a lab PC instead? Clone your project and set your connection string again before class: [A lab PC that rebooted](../week-10/lecture-notes.md#a-lab-pc-that-rebooted).

## The four parts, 10 points each

| 10 points | What it asks |
|---|---|
| **It runs** | from start to finish |
| **You show it** | what it does, by running it |
| **You explain one decision** | something you chose, and why |
| **You say what's next** | what you'd add or change, given another week |

**About ten minutes each**: around seven to present, and a few for questions from your instructor. Your instructor calls the order.

## 📋 Before class, don't forget

- ⚠️ **Print the grading sheet** from the lesson plan, one row per student
- ⚠️ **Check a laptop connects to the projector**, with whatever cable or adapter the room has
- ⚠️ **The `Final Presentation` rubric is set up in Canvas**: four criteria of 10, matching the table above
- 💡 **The order of presenters**, written down
